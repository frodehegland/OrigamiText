# Origami Text User Guide

*For Origami Text on iPhone, version 1.1. Origami Text also runs on iPad,
the Mac and Apple Vision Pro, which share your library through a community
folder (section 10).*

Origami Text is a reader for scholarly documents. Its home format is the
**Origami EPUB**: an ordinary EPUB, readable in any EPUB reader, that also
carries records about itself — who wrote it, where it was published, how to
cite it, what it cites. Because the book knows these things, a citation in
it is not just a mark on the page: tap it and the source appears.

The iPhone is the pocket end of Origami Text. It is for reading: the shelf,
the reader, highlights and notes. Writing, AI and the Views live on the Mac
(section 11).

## 1. Getting started

The first time Origami Text opens, it shows the **Introduction**, a short
welcome written by Frode Hegland and the Future Text Lab. The Introduction
cites this guide; tap that citation and choose **Open** to come here.

Both are always on the shelf's **Guide** tab: **Introduction**, **User
Guide** (this book), and a one-screen summary of how to read.

## 2. Bringing books in

Books arrive three ways, all on the phone itself — no Mac required:

- **From Files or a share sheet.** Open any `.epub` with Origami Text and it
  opens straight into reading, in Scroll view.
- **Open EPUB…** Tap the gear at the foot of the shelf and choose Open
  EPUB… to pick one or several books.
- **The community folder.** Tap the gear and choose Choose Community
  Folder… — the iCloud folder your group shares. Every EPUB in it joins the
  shelf, and new ones arrive whenever the app comes to the front. Books
  still downloading from iCloud are fetched and picked up a few seconds
  later.

A book republished with a newer edition replaces the old copy; its pin,
its set-aside standing and your highlights move to the new one.

## 3. The shelf

The shelf has three tabs at the top:

- **Articles** — every book, A to Z by title, pinned books first. Books you
  have set aside wait under **Set Aside** at the end; tap the header to
  show them.
- **Journals** — the journals and proceedings your books come from, the
  best stocked first, each with its count. Tap one to open it (section 4).
- **Guide** — the Introduction, this guide and the quick summary.

The foot of the shelf holds the **gear** (Open EPUB…, the community folder,
Settings…) and **Find**: type and Articles narrow to titles and authors
that match, Journals to names that match.

**To Read.** Hold your finger on the page for a second — in the margin or
the space between paragraphs, not on the words — and **To Read** appears:
the book is marked to come back to, at that paragraph. Holding on the
words still selects them; their card has **To Read** too. To Read books
lead every list, newest first, ahead of the pinned, with a teal bookmark
beside the title. Open one on any of your devices — the Mac, the iPad,
the iPhone or the headset — and it opens at the paragraph you marked.
It stays To Read until you take it off: the same press again, or touch
and hold the book in a list for **Remove from To Read**. A list's menu
can also mark a book To Read without a place.

**Pin and set aside.** Swipe a book to the right to pin it to the top of
every list; swipe to the left to set it aside. Or touch and hold the book
for the menu: To Read (or Remove from To Read), Pin, Set Aside (or Bring
Back), and **Note…**.

**A note on the whole book.** Note… writes a few words about the book
itself — no text selected, none needed. The note shows in quiet italics
under the book's author on the shelf. Save it empty to remove it.

## 4. A journal and its Map

A journal opens on its **Articles**: the list of its papers, pinned first.
Tap one to read it; the back chevron returns you to the journal.

**Map** lays the same papers out as cards on a large plane — the same
layout the Mac and the headset show, shared through the community folder.

- Pan with **two fingers**.
- **Tap** a card to lift it: it widens to show the full title, the authors
  and the abstract, with buttons to Pin and Set Aside. Tap it again to put
  it down.
- **Double-tap** a card to open the paper.
- **Drag** a card to move it; the place is saved for every device.
- Set-aside papers stay on the Map, faded, rather than vanishing.
- The Map's foot bar holds its own Find and the way back to the list.

## 5. Reading

Reading takes the whole screen. On iPhone the camera housing sits in a
black band of its own, so the page never flows around it. Everything you
need is in the **foot bar** at the bottom:

- **Documents** on the left goes back to the shelf. (In a cited book opened
  over the one you were reading, it is a **‹** and goes back to that book.)
- **Scroll | Focus** in the middle choose how the book reads.
- **◐** (the half circle) holds the looks: **Light** or **Dark**, the
  colour **Theme** — the Mac's full set, from sepia to the dyslexia- and
  Irlen-friendly palettes — and **Bionic Reading**, which sets the first
  half of every word in bold.
- **Aa** makes the words Bigger or Smaller, or resets them.

After four seconds without a touch, the foot bar — and in Focus the
heading bar above it — fades from the page so the words have it to
themselves. Tap where a bar was and they come back.

**Scroll** lays the whole text in one clean column.

**Focus** holds one section at a time — a heading and everything under it
until the next heading — with **Previous** and **Next** at the bottom and,
between them, the section's heading and where you are ("3 of 12"). A
second row appears above the foot bar with Focus's assists:

- **Section** — the whole section, heading and all.
- **Sentence** — one sentence at a time.
- **Paragraph** — one paragraph at a time.

  In Sentence and Paragraph, tap the right edge of the page to go on and
  the left edge to go back. The words between can be selected as anywhere
  else, for Highlight, Note… and To Read.

- **Word** — the text played one word after another in the middle of the
  screen. The **−** and **+** set the pace in words per minute (60 to
  800); **Close** returns to the page.

**Pinch for the outline.** Pinch in anywhere while reading and the book
folds into its outline — the section headings. Pinch out and it opens
again at the very spot you left. Tap a heading's name to open the book at
that section instead; the chevron beside it peeks at the section's words
without leaving the outline.

## 6. Selecting text

Touch and hold a word, then drag the handles to choose the words you want.
A card of verbs appears beside them:

- **Find** — looks for these words through the whole book (section 8).
- **Copy Citation** — copies the passage with a full citation to this
  exact spot in the book, ready to paste into Author or any document.
- **To Read** — marks the book to come back to, at this paragraph (or
  takes it off, when it is already To Read).
- **Highlight** — unfolds the kinds: Important, Quotable, Great, Disagree,
  Language Issue, Problematic, What is this?, Highlight and Strikethrough,
  each in its own colour. **Remove** clears the highlights on the selected
  words.
- **Note…** — writes a note on the selected words.
- **Copy** — copies the words alone.

Tap anywhere beside the card to put it away. Highlight names and colours
changed on the Mac read the same here.

Highlights and notes are kept beside the book, never inside it — the book
stays the author's; the annotations are yours.

## 7. Citations, notes and figures

**Citations.** Tap a citation — (Author 2026), [1] or a raised number —
and its source appears as a card: title, authors, year and venue, with:

- **Open** — when the cited document is on your shelf (this guide, cited
  by the Introduction, for one), it opens right there. The back chevron
  brings you back to where you were reading.
- **DOI** and **Web** — the source online, when it has them.
- **Online** — a web search for the work.
- **Copy BibTeX** — the full reference, ready for a bibliography.

**Notes.** Tap a note's mark — its raised number, [number] or ‡ — and the
note appears as a card. Notes in the **Fold [ ]** style open in place,
inside the sentence; tap again to fold them away.

**Figures.** Double-tap a figure to lift it into a card with its caption.
A figure made from a live view carries a small label in its corner: tap
it to open that view; touch and hold for Show Image and Show Reference.

**Jumps.** Links within a book — "see section 3", "Figure 2" — take you
there.

## 8. Finding words in a book

Select a word or phrase and choose **Find**. The book folds into its
outline, narrowed to the sections that contain the words, every
occurrence marked. The header shows what is sought and how many sections
answer. Tap any found paragraph and the reading opens right there. The
**×** in the header ends the search.

## 9. Settings

Tap the gear at the foot of the shelf and choose Settings…

- **Your Name** — as your papers carry it. It signs your highlights and
  notes.
- **Citations** — how citations read: **(Author Date)**, **[Number]** or
  **Superscript**. The tap, and the source it shows, are the same in every
  style.
- **Endnotes & Footnotes** — how note marks read: **Superscript** (the
  raised number the paper prints), **[Number]**, **Mark (‡)** or
  **Fold [ ]**. A raised number must mean exactly one thing, so choosing
  Superscript for citations moves notes off it, and the other way round.

- **On Loading** — what shows while a book arrives: a plain **Spinner**, or
  the **Animation** — its title, author and date floating in space, which
  a touch pushes and a shake scatters.

Citation and note choices are shared with the Mac and the headset.

## 10. Sharing between your devices

Choose the same iCloud community folder on every device and they read
from one library:

- Everything published into the folder from a Mac appears on the phone's
  shelf.
- **To Read** travels with its place: mark a paragraph on one device and
  the book opens there on the others.
- **Pin** and **Set Aside** travel: a book pinned on the Mac is pinned
  here within seconds, and the other way round.
- The journals' **Maps** share their layout.

## 11. What lives on the Mac

The Mac is where the full Origami Text lives: writing and exporting, AI
summaries and questions, the References page, the Views (Lineage,
Glossary, Ask and many more), Hypermedia spaces, folders, People and
Concepts, and importing papers from LaTeX, Word, PDF and other formats.
Its own user guide describes them all.
