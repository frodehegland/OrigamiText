# Origami Text User Guide

*For Origami Text on iPad, version 1.1. Origami Text also runs on iPhone,
the Mac and Apple Vision Pro, which share your library through a community
folder (section 11).*

Origami Text is a reader for scholarly documents. Its home format is the
**Origami EPUB**: an ordinary EPUB, readable in any EPUB reader, that also
carries records about itself — who wrote it, where it was published, how to
cite it, what it cites. Because the book knows these things, a citation in
it is not just a mark on the page: tap it and the source appears.

On iPad, Origami Text wears the Mac's shape: the same column on the left,
with the same places in the same order, so moving between the two needs no
relearning. Some places in the column are the Mac's alone for now; they say
so when you open them (section 12).

## 1. Getting started

The first time Origami Text opens, it shows the **Introduction**, a short
welcome written by Frode Hegland and the Future Text Lab. The Introduction
cites this guide; tap that citation and choose **Open** to come here.

Both are always at the foot of the column on the left: **Intro** opens the
Introduction, **User Guide** opens this book.

## 2. Bringing books in

Books arrive three ways, all on the iPad itself — no Mac required:

- **From Files or a share sheet.** Open any `.epub` with Origami Text and it
  opens straight into reading, in Scroll view.
- **Open EPUB…** Tap **+** at the top right and choose Open EPUB… to pick
  one or several books.
- **The community folder.** Tap **+** and choose Choose Community Folder…
  — the iCloud folder your group shares. Every EPUB in it joins the
  library, and new ones arrive whenever the app comes to the front. Books
  still downloading from iCloud are fetched and picked up a few seconds
  later.

A book republished with a newer edition replaces the old copy; its pin,
its set-aside standing and your highlights move to the new one.

## 3. The column on the left

The column is the Mac's sidebar. Its sections fold and unfold by their
headers, and stay as you leave them; XR and Views start folded.

**EPUB** — your library (section 4): Inbox, Pinned, Authors, Papers,
Journals, and a row with your surname for your own papers.

**Hypermedia**, **Folders** and **XR** — the Mac's network spaces, your
folders, and the headset's graphs and timelines.

**Views** — Annotations, People, Concept Space, Tracked Concepts, and the
Views: Ask, Glossary and **Lineage**. Lineage draws your papers as a
family tree of who cites whom across the years; tap a paper to read it.

At the foot, under a rule:

- **Intro** — the Introduction.
- **User Guide** — this guide.
- **Settings** — section 10.
- **Contact** — an email to frode@hegland.com with your suggestion.

When you open a book, the column steps aside so the page has the whole
screen; it comes back when you leave the book.

## 4. Your library

- **Inbox** — the newest arrivals: everything added in the last 30 days,
  and never fewer than the 20 newest, newest first. A book stands in
  **bold** until you open it, and the Inbox row itself is bold while any
  are waiting.
- **Pinned** — the books you have pinned.
- **Authors** — everyone who wrote what is in your library, by surname,
  with how many of their papers you have. Tap a name for their papers.
- **Papers** — every book, by **Title** or by **Date** (newest first),
  pinned books first. Books you have set aside wait under **Set Aside** at
  the end; tap the header to show them.
- **Journals** — the journals and proceedings your books come from, the
  best stocked first. Tap one to open it (section 5).
- **Your surname** — the papers you wrote. Type your name in Settings, as
  your papers carry it; until it is set, the row reads "Mine".

**Find**, at the top of each list, narrows it as you type: books by title
and author, Authors by name, Journals by name.

**To Read.** Hold your finger on the page for a second — in the margin or
the space between paragraphs, not on the words — and **To Read** appears:
the book is marked to come back to, at that paragraph. Holding on the
words still selects them; their card has **To Read** too. To Read books
lead every list, newest first, ahead of the pinned, with a teal bookmark
beside the title. Open one on any of your devices — the Mac, the iPad,
the iPhone or the headset — and it opens at the paragraph you marked.
It stays To Read until you take it off: the same press again, or touch
and hold the book in a list for **Remove from To Read**. A list's menu
can also mark a book To Read without a place.

**Pin and set aside.** Swipe a book to the right to pin it to the top of
every list; swipe to the left to set it aside. Or touch and hold the book
for the menu: To Read (or Remove from To Read), Pin, Set Aside (or Bring
Back), and **Note…**.

**A note on the whole book.** Note… writes a few words about the book
itself — no text selected, none needed. The note shows in quiet italics
under the book's author in every list. Save it empty to remove it.

## 5. A journal and its Map

A journal opens on its **Articles**: the list of its papers, pinned first.
Tap one to read it; the back chevron returns you to the journal.

**Map** lays the same papers out as cards on a large plane — the same
layout the Mac and the headset show, shared through the community folder.

- Pan with **two fingers**.
- **Tap** a card to lift it: it widens to show the full title, the authors
  and the abstract, with buttons to Pin and Set Aside. Tap it again to put
  it down.
- **Double-tap** a card to open the paper.
- **Drag** a card to move it; the place is saved for every device.
- Set-aside papers stay on the Map, faded, rather than vanishing.
- The Map's foot bar holds its own Find and the way back to the list.

## 6. Reading

Reading takes the whole screen. Everything you need is in the **foot bar**
at the bottom:

- **Documents** on the left goes back to your library. (In a cited book
  opened over the one you were reading, it is a **‹** and goes back to that
  book.)
- **Scroll | Horizontal | Focus** in the middle choose how the book reads.
- **◐** (the half circle) holds the looks: **Light** or **Dark**, the
  colour **Theme** — the Mac's full set, from sepia to the dyslexia- and
  Irlen-friendly palettes — and **Bionic Reading**, which sets the first
  half of every word in bold.
- **Aa** makes the words Bigger or Smaller, or resets them.

After four seconds without a touch, the foot bar fades from the page so
the words have it to themselves. On iPhone the page then runs edge to edge,
with a black pill at the foot that mirrors the Dynamic Island at the top —
tap it (or anywhere along the foot) and the bar comes back.

**Scroll** lays the whole text in one column, held to a book's width and
centred.

**Horizontal** stands the sections side by side like the pages of a
spread: two columns in portrait, three in landscape. Swipe and the spread
steps one column along. The book's title rides the foot bar.

**Focus** holds one section at a time — a heading and everything under it
until the next heading. Tap the right edge of the page for the next, the
left edge for the one before. A second row appears above the foot bar with Focus's assists:

- **Section** — the whole section, heading and all.
- **Paragraph** — one paragraph at a time.
- **Sentence** — one sentence at a time.

  In Section, Sentence and Paragraph, tap the right edge of the page to go
  on and the left edge to go back. The words between can be selected as anywhere
  else, for Highlight, Note… and To Read.

- **Word** — the text played one word after another in the middle of the
  screen. The **−** and **+** set the pace in words per minute (60 to
  800); **Close** returns to the page.

**Pinch for the outline.** Pinch in anywhere while reading and the book
folds into its outline — the section headings. Pinch out and it opens
again at the very spot you left. Tap a heading's name to open the book at
that section instead; the chevron beside it peeks at the section's words
without leaving the outline.

## 7. Selecting text

Touch and hold a word, then drag the handles to choose the words you want
(or drag across them with a trackpad or Apple Pencil). A card of verbs
appears beside them:

- **Find** — looks for these words through the whole book (section 9).
- **Copy Citation** — copies the passage with a full citation to this
  exact spot in the book, ready to paste into Author or any document.
- **To Read** — marks the book to come back to, at this paragraph (or
  takes it off, when it is already To Read).
- **Highlight** — unfolds the kinds: Important, Quotable, Great, Disagree,
  Language Issue, Problematic, What is this?, Highlight and Strikethrough,
  each in its own colour. **Remove** clears the highlights on the selected
  words.
- **Note…** — writes a note on the selected words.
- **Copy** — copies the words alone.

Tap anywhere beside the card to put it away. Highlight names and colours
changed on the Mac read the same here.

Highlights and notes are kept beside the book, never inside it — the book
stays the author's; the annotations are yours.

## 8. Citations, notes and figures

**Citations.** Tap a citation — (Author 2026), [1] or a raised number —
and its source appears as a card: title, authors, year and venue, with:

- **Open** — when the cited document is in your library (this guide,
  cited by the Introduction, for one), it opens right there. The back
  chevron brings you back to where you were reading.
- **Acquire** — when the cited work is not on your shelf: lists it in the
  community folder's To Acquire, which the Mac shows, so you can get it
  there. **Listed to Acquire** shows once it is asked for.
- **DOI** and **Web** — the source online, when it has them.
- **Online** — a web search for the work.
- **Copy BibTeX** — the full reference, ready for a bibliography.

**Notes.** Tap a note's mark — its raised number, [number] or ‡ — and the
note appears as a card. Notes in the **Fold [ ]** style open in place,
inside the sentence; tap again to fold them away.

**Figures.** Double-tap a figure and it opens full screen on black. Tap once
for the **X** and the caption; pinch or double-tap to look closer; tap the X
or swipe down to close.
A figure made from a live view carries a small label in its corner: tap
it to open that view; touch and hold for Show Image and Show Reference.

**Jumps.** Links within a book — "see section 3", "Figure 2" — take you
there.

## 9. Finding things

**In your library**, use the Find field at the top of any list (section
4).

**In a book**, select a word or phrase and choose **Find**. The book folds
into its outline, narrowed to the sections that contain the words, every
occurrence marked. The header shows what is sought and how many sections
answer. Tap any found paragraph and the reading opens right there. The
**×** in the header ends the search.

## 10. Settings

**Settings**, at the foot of the column:

- **Your Name** — as your papers carry it: first name, middle name, last name
  ("Frode Alexander Hegland"). It follows your Apple ID to your Mac, iPhone,
  iPad and Vision Pro. It names your own papers' row
  in the column and signs your highlights and notes.
- **Citations** — how citations read: **(Author Date)**, **[Number]** or
  **Superscript**. The tap, and the source it shows, are the same in every
  style.
- **Endnotes & Footnotes** — how note marks read: **Superscript** (the
  raised number the paper prints), **[Number]**, **Mark (‡)** or
  **Fold [ ]**. A raised number must mean exactly one thing, so choosing
  Superscript for citations moves notes off it, and the other way round.

- **Focus Type** — how Focus sets its words: the face (**Iowan Old Style**,
  the default; **New York**; **Hoefler Text**; **Baskerville**), **Justify**,
  and **Rare & Historical Ligatures** — ct, st and their kin, in Hoefler Text
  and Baskerville. Focus is always hyphenated, never leaves a lone word on a
  paragraph's last line, sets figures in old style and, in Iowan Old Style
  and New York, acronyms such as EPUB in true small capitals. The book's
  characters are never changed, so highlights and notes still find their
  place.
- **Reading Screen** — **Pill** (the default): the page runs to the top,
  the toolbar fades after four quiet seconds and a black pill at the foot
  brings it back. **Framed**: a black band at the top; the toolbar
  fades the same way, and a tap where it was brings it back.
- **On Loading** — what shows while a book arrives: a plain **Spinner**, or
  the **Animation** — its title, author and date floating in space, which
  a touch pushes and a shake scatters.

Citation and note choices are shared with the Mac and the headset.

## 11. Sharing between your devices

Choose the same iCloud community folder on every device and they read
from one library:

- Everything published into the folder from a Mac appears in the iPad's
  library.
- **To Read** travels with its place: mark a paragraph on one device and
  the book opens there on the others.
- **Pin** and **Set Aside** travel: a book pinned on the Mac is pinned
  here within seconds, and the other way round.
- The journals' **Maps** share their layout.

## 12. What lives on the Mac

These places keep their row in the column so the iPad and the Mac stay
the same shape, but for now they open on the Mac alone: Hypermedia spaces,
Folders, the XR Graphs and Timelines, Annotations, People, Concept Space,
Tracked Concepts, Ask, Glossary and Edit Views.

The Mac is also where writing and exporting, AI summaries and questions,
the References page and importing papers from LaTeX, Word, PDF and other
formats live. Its own user guide describes them all.
